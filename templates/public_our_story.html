Our Story, Our Mission

The Hafemann Family

Most of us have heard the expression “don’t waste the pain.” Kent and Pamela were both raised in broken homes. In our youth, our natural parents were married sixteen times to different people, and we both moved from place to place many times. We suffered significant trauma, abandonment, and upheaval. The impact of these life circumstances was significant beyond our young imagination.

I was 24 and Pamela was 23 when we started discussing the idea of marriage. We both were scared spitless, since by all measures of life calculus, the odds of living successfully were minimal at best. We were in college, broke, and clueless about what it takes to remain in a marriage and be happy. But we had stars in our eyes, lots of hope, and a mutual commitment to live life differently. We were married on March 18, 1978.

Having been married about 12 years, our marriage was in trouble. We had three children and lots of responsibility. I was a VP in a large real estate investment firm and very active as a leader in our church. Pamela was mostly a stay-at-home mom raising our children. But she too was a leader in our church, and to help with the bills, she took a part-time sales job working for a lighting company. We both were college graduates, and most who knew us considered us to be smart, successful, and living the dream. But our marriage was falling apart, and we couldn’t figure out why.

We engaged a counselor and sought help from the church and our friends. Yet despite our best efforts, we could not figure out why our marriage was failing, until in 1996, together with Max Thompson, we invested in a powerful life training program, and it changed our lives forever.

The tools we were given in this training helped us uncover and manage the crippling emotional luggage we carried around. We realized how the hard parts of our personal stories were impacting our relationships at home and in the workplace, and with this new-found knowledge, we found common ground to heal our hearts, our home, and to grow individually.

A Journey I Never Expected

When I first attended the training, I really didn’t know what I was getting into. A friend had encouraged me to go—actually, begged me to go—and eventually I agreed.

I had no idea how much that decision would mean to me.

As I sat in that room with people from all walks of life, I began to realize something important. Every one of us had experiences, beliefs, and patterns worth examining. We all had opportunities to grow, to understand ourselves better, and to become more of the person we wanted to be.

Throughout the experience, I began looking at myself in ways I never had before. I recognized things I wanted to change, discovered feelings I hadn’t fully understood, and even found places in my life where I needed to forgive myself.

One of the most meaningful discoveries was the importance of communication—not simply knowing what to say, but learning to speak honestly from the heart, to listen more openly, and to connect with others in a deeper way.

By the end of those first three days, I felt happier than I could remember feeling in my entire life. And what made the experience even more remarkable was seeing the joy and newfound understanding in the people around me.

Something had changed for me.

I knew I wanted to be part of helping others experience that kind of discovery.

I returned as an assistant and continued serving in that role for years before eventually becoming a facilitator. What began as something a friend persuaded me to attend became one of the most meaningful parts of my life.

Today, I find tremendous satisfaction in helping people recognize their own potential, discover new perspectives, and become more of who they want to be.

And if I could give everyone the opportunity to experience this kind of personal discovery, I would.

That’s how much it has meant to me.
